    Comparative Evaluation of Best-Fit Nesting Heuristics for FDM 3D Printing Batch Scheduling: A Paired Experimental Study with Sorting Decomposition
    Galih Prakoso, Abdulrahman Mushabab Al-Ahmari, Anis Gharbi, and 1 more author
    Rapid Prototyping Journal, Nov 2026
    Published online 12 November 2026 (early access, ahead of print). Author accepted manuscript.
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    Purpose: This study investigates the contribution of sorting pre-processing and placement criterion selection to FDM batch scheduling performance, addressing a fundamental attribution ambiguity in prior nesting literature where these two factors have never been empirically decomposed. Design/methodology/approach: Five algorithms are compared: a Naive sequential baseline, a Sorted Naive intermediate baseline (enabling sorting-vs-placement decomposition), and three Best-Fit variants: Original (BF), height-penalized (BF-H), and volume-grouped (BF-G). Algorithms are evaluated on 210-part samples drawn from a 5,335-part preprocessing pipeline applied to the Thingi10K benchmark across 25 paired experimental runs. Performance is assessed on batch count, total processing time, and platform utilization using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Repeated Measures ANOVA, and pairwise post-hoc comparisons. Findings: Sorting alone significantly worsens batch count relative to the unsorted baseline (+2.84%, W = 52.0, p = 0.025), establishing placement logic as the primary performance driver. All three BF variants achieve 18.76 to 19.87% batch reductions versus the sorted baseline (p < 0.001). All BF variants are statistically equivalent to one another (all pairwise p > 0.05); BF-G achieves the lowest processing time (19,664 min, -7.09% vs. Naive) and highest platform utilization (66.91%). Repeated Measures ANOVA confirms omnibus significance (F(4,96) = 244.04 and 171.42, p < 0.001). Originality: This paper presents the first empirical sorting-vs-placement decomposition in FDM nesting optimization, introduces a preprocessing pipeline yielding 5,335 validated parts from Thingi10K, and provides a statistically rigorous paired evaluation framework with effect-size reporting, a methodological standard absent from prior FDM nesting literature.
